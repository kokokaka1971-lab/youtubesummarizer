YouTube Summary Not Working: What to Check

Almost every failure in this category comes from the same place, and it is not the summarizer. Six causes, in the order they are worth checking.

Try a video Full FAQ

The FAQ covers the common questions; this page is for when something specifically did not work. There are two kinds of failure, and they need separating before you can fix anything. Loud failures tell you something went wrong. Quiet ones hand you a summary that looks fine and is not.

The quiet kind is more common than people realise, which is why the last section matters most.

Start here: does the video have captions?

This accounts for the large majority of outright failures. Every mainstream summarizer reads the caption track, so no captions means no input.

Check it in ten seconds: open the video's description, expand it, and look for "Show transcript". If it is not there, no caption track exists yet.

Recent uploads: usually just wait

Captions take a while to generate, and longer for long videos. A talk posted this morning may not be summarisable until this afternoon, and a four-hour stream replay can take longer still.

Google documents a related constraint for NotebookLM — videos uploaded within the previous 72 hours may not be importable — which reflects the same underlying pipeline rather than a quirk of one tool.

So if the upload is fresh, the answer is almost always patience rather than a different tool.

When the video is private or restricted

If a tool cannot reach the video, it cannot read it. Private videos, members-only content and most age-restricted material are outside what a summarizer can retrieve.

Unlisted videos usually work, because the URL is the access. That covers a lot of lecture recordings and internal training material shared as unlisted links.

The failure that does not announce itself

This is the important one. On a long video, some tools return a confident summary of the first portion and give no indication the rest was never read.

Every sentence in it is accurate about the part that was processed, so nothing reads as wrong. You simply end up with a summary of the opening third, filed in your head as a summary of the whole thing.

The check takes two seconds: compare the final timestamp with the video's runtime. If a 2:40:00 video's last citation is at 0:48:00, you have your answer. More detail in summarizing long videos.

Browser and connection problems

Worth ruling out early, because they look identical to a tool failure and are trivially fixable.

An aggressive content blocker can prevent the page from reaching the service at all. A corporate network may block the request. And a stale cached copy of the page can behave oddly after an update — a hard refresh resolves that one.

The quick test is another browser, or the same browser in a private window with extensions disabled. If it works there, the problem is local and the tool is fine.

When the summary is thin rather than absent

A short, vague summary of a long video is usually the video's fault rather than the tool's, and it is worth telling the two apart.

If the content is visual — a screen recording, a whiteboard derivation, a performance — the transcript contains narration around work you cannot see. There is nothing to summarize because the meaning was never in the words.

The quick predictor: if you could follow the video with your eyes closed it will summarize well; if you could follow it with the sound off it will not.

When terms come out wrong

This one is worth separating from the failures above, because nothing actually broke — the pipeline worked exactly as designed on input that was already wrong.

Not a failure of the summary so much as of the captions underneath it. Automatic transcription is weakest on technical vocabulary and proper nouns, and those errors flow through unchanged.

If a term looks wrong for the subject, open its timestamp and listen. That is the only reliable correction, and it takes ten seconds — see how accurate auto-captions are for why this clusters where it does.

When it worked yesterday and not today

Occasionally a video that summarised fine last week fails now, which feels like a tool problem and usually is not.

Uploaders can change a video's visibility, disable captions, or replace a recording. A talk that was public during a conference sometimes becomes unlisted or private afterwards, and members-only conversions are common on channels that monetise back catalogues.

Google's documentation on adding video sources notes a related consequence: if a video is deleted or made private, material derived from it gets removed downstream too. The video is the dependency, and it can change under you.

A checklist

  1. Does "Show transcript" appear under the video? If not, that is your answer.
  2. Was it uploaded in the last few hours? Wait and retry.
  3. Is it private, members-only or age-restricted? Not retrievable.
  4. Does the video have speech at all? Music and silent recordings cannot be summarised.
  5. Did you get output? Check the last timestamp against the runtime before reading it.
  6. Do the technical terms look right? Verify odd ones at their timestamps.

When it is genuinely the tool

Occasionally it is. If a video has a visible transcript, is public, is not brand new, and still fails repeatedly, that is worth reporting rather than working around.

Send us the link — a URL that reproducibly fails is far more useful than a description of the failure, and it is usually something specific about how that video's captions are structured.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it say there's no transcript?

Because no caption track exists — usually a recent upload where captions have not generated, a video with no speech, or captions disabled by the uploader. Only the first fixes itself with time.

Does a failed video use up my daily allowance?

No. If there was no transcript to read, nothing was summarised and your count is untouched.

Can you summarize private or age-restricted videos?

Private and age-restricted, generally no — the video cannot be retrieved. Unlisted videos usually work, since the URL itself is the access.

The summary seems to miss the end of the video. Why?

Check the final timestamp against the runtime. If it stops well short, the transcript was truncated or the tail was skimmed — a common and silent failure on long videos.

Why is the summary so short for a long video?

Often correct behaviour — a summary answers a fixed question and should not grow with runtime. If it is short and vague rather than short and specific, the video may be visual rather than spoken.

Check the transcript first

Most failures are a missing caption track. Look for "Show transcript" before anything else.
