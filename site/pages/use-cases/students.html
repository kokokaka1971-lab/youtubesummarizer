The problem with rewatching

Rewatching a lecture feels like studying. It mostly isn't. You sit through the eighty minutes you already understood to reach the four you didn't, and the familiarity of hearing it again gets mistaken for knowing it.

What you actually need is a map: the shape of the lecture, where each idea starts, and a way to jump directly to the bit that didn't land. Then the rewatching you do is the four minutes that matter. That is what running a lecture through a YouTube summarizer gets you — not a replacement for the lecture, a table of contents for it.

  1. 1

    Run each lecture once, early

    Do it the same day, while you still remember which part lost you. Paste the link, take the chapter map, and you have a skeleton of the whole lecture in under a minute.

  2. 2

    Mark the gaps, don't fill them yet

    Read the takeaways. The ones you couldn't have written yourself are your gaps. Each has a timestamp next to it — that's your rewatch list, and it's usually five minutes, not ninety.

  3. 3

    Export into wherever your notes live

    Copy the full notes as Markdown and paste them into Notion or Obsidian under the week's heading. Headings and links survive the paste, so the timestamps still work months later.

  4. 4

    Revise from the map, not the video

    At exam time you're reading a structured page per lecture with a link back to the source for anything that's gone fuzzy. That's the whole point: recall practice first, video only as a fallback.

What matters most

The summarizer features students actually use

Chapter maps

A lecture broken into its actual sections, each linked to the second it starts. Matches how a syllabus is structured far better than a wall of transcript.

Lectures in another language

Studying abroad, or the best course on your topic is in Japanese? Summarize it into English and keep the technical terms in both.

Clean Markdown export

Straight into Notion, Obsidian, or a shared revision doc. No reformatting, no broken links, no copy-paste soup.

One thing worth saying

A summary is a study aid, not a substitute for the course. It's very good at telling you where the thing you don't understand lives, and it can't do the understanding for you. Use it to spend your rewatching well, not to skip it entirely.
