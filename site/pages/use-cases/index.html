What changes between one use case and the next

Everyone pastes the same link into the same box. What differs is which of the four outputs does the work, and that turns out to map closely onto why you were watching in the first place.

If you're learning something — a student with recorded lectures, anyone working through a course — the chapter map is the useful part. It gives you the shape of the material and a list of timestamps to go back to, which is what turns a ninety-minute video into fifteen minutes of targeted rewatching.

If you're deciding what to read — triaging a conference playlist, scanning a channel before you commit — the TL;DR does almost everything. You're not trying to learn the content yet, you're trying to find out whether it's worth learning, and one paragraph per video answers that forty times faster than watching.

If you're going to be quoted on it — citing a talk, sending a claim to your team, writing something public — the timestamps matter more than the summary. Being able to link the exact second a thing was said is the difference between a note and a source.

And if you're passing it on, the takeaways are the deliverable. Six bullets in a channel with links for anyone who wants to go deeper beats a two-hour link that nobody opens.
