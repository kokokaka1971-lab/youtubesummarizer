One person watches, everyone benefits

The economics of a recorded talk are strange: the cost of watching it scales with the number of people who watch, but the value is in the four ideas it contains. A team of eight watching a ninety-minute keynote spends twelve hours to extract something that reads in ninety seconds.

The fix isn't making people watch faster. It's making sure the extraction happens once, in a format that stays useful and links back to the source for whoever wants to go deeper. One person runs the YouTube video through the summarizer; everybody else reads six bullets.

  1. 1

    Summarize into the shared workspace

    Anyone on the team runs the video; the result lands in shared history where it's searchable by everyone. No one else has to wonder whether it's already been done.

  2. 2

    Post the takeaways, keep the timestamps

    Six bullets in the channel, with links into the video for each. People who need the detail click through to the exact minute; everyone else got what they needed from the message.

  3. 3

    Standardise the format

    A house summary format means every competitor teardown, every conference talk, every customer call recording comes back with the same headings. Team notes become comparable instead of a pile of individual styles.

  4. 4

    Wire it into what you already use

    The API turns this into a step in a pipeline: new video in a watched playlist, summary posted to a channel or filed in your wiki, no human in the loop until there's something worth reading.
