How it works

How to Summarize a YouTube Video

Paste a link, pick a depth, take the notes with you — about sixty seconds start to finish, free and without an account. Here is exactly what happens in between, and what the tool cannot do.

Try it now See the features
  1. 1

    Step 1: Paste the YouTube link

    The URL is parsed the moment you stop typing. Watch links, youtu.be short links, Shorts, embeds, live replays and bare video ids all resolve to the same eleven-character id.

    As soon as that id is valid, the video's real title, channel and thumbnail are fetched and shown — so you know you've got the right video before spending a summary on it.

  2. 2

    Step 2: The transcript is fetched

    Summarization runs on the video's transcript, with timings attached to every line. Where YouTube has captions — human or auto-generated — those are used directly. Where it doesn't, Pro transcribes the audio instead.

    The timings matter as much as the words. They're what makes it possible to attach a timestamp to every chapter and claim later, and they're carried through the whole pipeline rather than discarded after the first step.

    If no transcript can be reached at all, you get told that plainly. You will never get a confident summary of a video nobody could read.

  3. 3

    Step 3: The AI reads it, rather than skimming

    The transcript goes to a large language model with the timing markers intact and a brief that's deliberately narrow: find the actual argument, keep what's specific, drop what's filler, and anchor each section to where it begins.

    The result comes back as structured data — a TL;DR, a list of takeaways, chapters with start times, and headed note sections — rather than as prose that then has to be picked apart. That's why switching between the four views is instant: they were all produced in the same pass.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Copy, export or share the summary

    Copy it as Markdown, download it as a file, or send the link. Notes that stay inside the tool that made them aren't much use, so the export is plain Markdown with real heading levels and working links — the kind that survives a paste into Notion, Obsidian, or a document.

    Your recent summaries stay a click away in the sidebar. Without an account they live in your browser's local storage and nowhere else.

Honest limits

What a YouTube summarizer can't do

Worth knowing before you rely on it.

It can't summarize a video with no words in it

Music videos, silent timelapses, and footage where the meaning is entirely visual have nothing for a transcript-based tool to work with. You'll get told, rather than given a summary invented from the title.

It can't read a private video

Unlisted videos work if you have the link. Private ones don't — we can only reach what your link can reach.

It can't tell you whether the video is right

A summary reflects what was said, not whether it's true. If a video is confidently wrong, the summary will be a faithful account of a confidently wrong argument. The timestamps are there so you can go and judge for yourself.

Auto-generated captions carry their own errors

Where YouTube's automatic captions mis-hear a name or a technical term, that error flows downstream. Heavy accents, poor audio, and specialist vocabulary are where this shows up most — another reason every claim keeps its timestamp.
