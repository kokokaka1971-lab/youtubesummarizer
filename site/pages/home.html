    The basics

    What is a YouTube video summarizer?

    A YouTube video summarizer reads a video's transcript and turns it into something you can read in a fraction of the time. You paste a link, the AI works through what was actually said, and you get back the argument in condensed form — a short TL;DR, the key points, and a breakdown of the video's sections.

    The reason it works is arithmetic, not magic. Summarizing isn't a shortcut so much as a correction: it removes the padding that exists only because the medium is speech.

    A summary you can't verify is just a confident paragraph.

    What separates a good summary from a bad one

    Plenty of tools labelled "summarizer" reformat the caption file and hand it back. That's a transcript, not a summary — the same five thousand words with paragraph breaks. Four things have to happen in between.

    1. 01

      Cut the disfluency, keep the meaning

      Stripping the "um"s is easy. The hard part is that speakers repeat themselves for emphasis, and that looks identical to repeating themselves because they lost the thread.

    2. 02

      Recover the structure

      The sections were real — the speaker had an outline. They're just not marked in the text, so they have to be inferred from the content itself rather than found.

    3. 03

      Separate claims from scaffolding

      Most of a talk is setup, analogy and recap. Telling a claim apart from a vivid way of restating the previous claim is most of the work.

    4. 04

      Keep every claim tied to its moment

      The one most tools skip. Ours links each chapter and takeaway back to its timestamp, so checking a claim costs one click instead of a rewatch. Why that matters.

    When you should still watch the video

    A summary tells you what a video said, not whether it's true, and it can't carry a demonstration, a visual proof, or a performance. Use it to decide what deserves your attention and to find the part you need — then watch that part properly.

    FAQ

    YouTube video summarizer questions

    The ten things people ask before pasting a first link — captions, length, accuracy and what happens to your data.

    Read every question
    Do I need an account to try it?

    No. Paste a link and go. An account only matters once you want history that follows you between devices, or the limits lifted.

    What if the video has no captions?

    If YouTube has no transcript — auto-generated or human — we transcribe the audio ourselves on Pro. On Free you'll get a clear message rather than a summary of nothing.

    How long can a video be?

    Thirty minutes on Free. On Pro there's no ceiling — eight-hour conference streams and full audiobook readings both work, they just take a little longer.

    Is it accurate?

    Every claim stays anchored to the timestamp it came from, so you can check it in one click. That's the honest answer: a summary is a starting point, and we make verifying it fast rather than asking you to take it on faith.

    Do you store the videos or my summaries?

    We never store video files. Without an account, your recent summaries live in this browser's local storage and nowhere else — clearing them here clears them, full stop.

    Can I use this for private or unlisted videos?

    Unlisted links work if you have the URL. Private videos don't — we can only read what your link can reach.

    How do I summarize a YouTube video for free?

    Copy the video's URL, paste it into the box at the top of this page, and press Summarize. You'll have a TL;DR, key takeaways and timestamped chapters in a few seconds. There's no account, no card, and no trial countdown — the free plan covers five videos a day, every day.

    Can it summarize long videos and podcasts?

    Yes. Thirty minutes is the ceiling on the free plan; on Pro there isn't one. Two-hour podcasts, eight-hour conference streams and full lecture recordings all work — longer videos simply take a little longer to process, because there's more transcript to read.

    Does it work on YouTube Shorts and live replays?

    Both. Any youtube.com/shorts/ or /live/ URL resolves the same way a normal watch link does, along with youtu.be short links, embeds, and a bare video ID pasted on its own.

    How is this different from pasting a transcript into ChatGPT?

    Mostly the timestamps. Copying a transcript into a chat window throws away the timings, so the summary you get back can't be traced to any particular moment — you either trust it or rewatch. Here the timings survive the whole pipeline, so every chapter and claim links back to the second it came from. You also skip the fetching and pasting, and get four depths from one run rather than re-prompting for each.
